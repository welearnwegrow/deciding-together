Shaping proposals

In short. Most decisions go wrong before the decision is made: the question is framed too narrowly, the loudest voice sets the options, or a weak proposal reaches the vote. The methods on this page don't make the decision. They improve what gets decided. Use them in roughly this order: frame the question, open up, understand each other, narrow down, shape the proposal, then test support. When the proposal is ready, hand it to whichever decision structure your group uses.

The shape below comes from Sam Kaner and colleagues. A group starts with familiar opinions. When it opens up to new ideas, it usually goes through an uncomfortable stretch of confusion and disagreement, which they call the groan zone, before ideas come together again. Groups that rush to close in that stretch tend to settle for the familiar option. Groups that work through it more often reach a proposal everyone can support.

Short on time? A one-hour version

  1. Frame the question and say how it will be decided (5 minutes).
  2. A go-around: each person says what matters to them (10 minutes).
  3. Nominal group technique: ideas written alone, shared in turn (15 minutes).
  4. Sort the ideas into themes and agree on three criteria (10 minutes).
  5. Draft a proposal together from the ideas that best meet the criteria (10 minutes).
  6. Test support for the draft with the gradients of agreement (10 minutes).
